# LLM Narrative Authoring Instructions

**Scope:** All LLM-generated character names, dialogue, descriptions, and provisional worldbuilding for Ambition.

**Purpose:** Generate provisional content that supports a coherent, inhabited, playable world. Human editing and polishing will happen later. Prioritize useful content, continuity, and character differentiation over literary polish.

## 1. Ground generation in existing content

Before generating new narrative content:

1. Read the relevant character, location, and story definitions.
2. Inspect nearby dialogue and the names already used in that community.
3. Identify established facts, provisional story ideas, and unresolved questions.
4. Determine what the characters involved plausibly know.
5. Identify connections to existing people, events, institutions, and conflicts.

Do not invent replacements for established facts.

Do not turn provisional brainstorms into canonical facts unless the task authorizes it.

## 2. Generate characters from circumstances

Establish the character's circumstances before writing their personality or dialogue.

Consider their work, relationships, current activity, local concerns, and exposure to recent events.

Use only the details that are relevant. Do not invent elaborate biographies for minor NPCs.

Avoid constructing characters from stock archetype descriptions such as "enigmatic wanderer," "wise mentor," or "sarcastic rogue" unless the setting specifically calls for them.

Do not give every character a defining quirk.

## 3. Naming procedure

Do not optimize names for how evocative, mysterious, memorable, or fantasy-like they sound.

Derive candidate names from the established naming conventions of the location, community, family, or character.

Consider ordinary names alongside distinctive ones.

Avoid repeatedly selecting familiar LLM-generated naming patterns.

**Naming watchlist:** `Voss`

Treat this as a warning against automatic selection, not an absolute prohibition. An intentionally selected name such as *Elara Voss* may be appropriate when it is painfully aware that the llmy name was chosen on purpose.

If a candidate matches the watchlist, compare it against alternatives and select it only when there is a character-specific reason.

Do not automatically substitute another fashionable fantasy surname.

Avoid giving every character a name that encodes their occupation, personality, or narrative function.

## 4. Dialogue generation procedure

For each conversation:

1. Determine what the speaker is doing when approached.
2. Determine what they want from the exchange.
3. Determine what they know about the subject.
4. Determine how they relate to the other participant.
5. Write the exchange as speech appropriate to that situation.

Prefer concrete concerns over generalized observations.

Characters may misunderstand, refuse to answer, change the subject, give incomplete information, or simply have nothing interesting to say.

Do not make every exchange funny, profound, informative, or narratively consequential.

Do not impose a uniform number of dialogue branches on unrelated characters.

## 5. Avoid convergent LLM writing patterns

During revision, examine the output for:

- Formulaic rhetorical reversals and contrasts.
- Aphorisms or concluding moral statements.
- Repeatedly symmetrical sentence structures.
- Stock metaphors used to explain occupations or personalities.
- Unnecessary exposition.
- Generic expressions of importance or significance.
- Excessive agreement, reassurance, or praise.
- Dialogue that resolves too neatly.
- Unmotivated cleverness and interchangeable jokes.
- Multiple characters using the same conversational rhythm.
- Repetitive character names, occupations, histories, or personality traits.

These are pattern warnings, not universal prohibitions.

A theatrical character may speak theatrically. A philosopher may discuss philosophy. A comedian may deliberately make jokes.

Preserve stylistic choices when they follow from established characterization and circumstances.

Do not replace all expressive writing with terse, generic speech.

## 6. Connect the world without overexplaining it

Use established events to create plausible consequences for different residents.

A gate opening might affect freight schedules, family visits, employment, security, or local prices.

Different characters should have different knowledge and priorities.

Connections to major characters and plot events should emerge when relevant to the speaker.

Do not mention major plot characters merely to make dialogue appear connected to the main story.

Allow rumors, disagreement, ignorance, and mistaken beliefs, while preserving the distinction between character knowledge and established world facts.

## 7. Check the surrounding cast

After generating multiple characters or conversations, review them together.

Check whether:

- They have different concerns and conversational habits.
- They possess plausible and distinct information.
- Their names exhibit excessive repetition.
- Their conversations follow the same structural template.
- They repeatedly deliver similar jokes or observations.
- Their relationships and local references remain consistent.

Revise clusters of similar characters. Do not make arbitrary changes solely to maximize difference.

## 8. Preserve functionality

Do not alter existing dialogue identifiers, quest commands, progression conditions, or required lines unless explicitly authorized.

Preserve established character relationships and facts.

When introducing new content, prefer additions that can be revised independently without disrupting gameplay.

## 9. Final review

Before delivering generated narrative content:

- Check continuity against the available source material.
- Remove filler that contributes neither characterization, setting, gameplay, nor ordinary local life.
- Review obvious repeated structures and names.
- Check that dialogue is plausible when spoken aloud.
- Preserve deliberate humor, strangeness, and stylistic variation.

Do not claim the writing has become natural, human-like, or free of LLM patterns.

**Output the requested content. Do not include a self-assessment, checklist report, explanation of your creative process, or references to these instructions unless explicitly requested.**
