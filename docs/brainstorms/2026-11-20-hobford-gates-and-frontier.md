# Hobford, gates, and the frontier: ideas from November 19-20, 2026

**Status:** Brainstorm record, not canon. This adds to `story_and_world_directions_2026-11-18.md`. Keep the intended story route coherent, but treat its order and details as provisional. `[User]` means the maintainer proposed, favored, or specified the idea; it does not mean it is final. `[Assistant]` means a suggestion without clear adoption. Preserve independent stories; do not join them only because they occur nearby.

## Hobford and its return visit

- **[User]** Hobford is a relatively ordinary rural town in a low-magic fantasy dimension. Swords, armor, knights, monsters in the woods, caves, ruins, and optional dungeons belong here. It is not the dimension where magic goes wild.
- **[User]** Gate commerce changes local life. The blacksmith fears losing young adventurers to imported laser swords. **Favored detail:** he reluctantly sells laser swords himself, hates them, and they are his best-selling product. A recovery inn or hotel serves gate travelers.
- **[User]** Goblins can be native residents with ordinary jobs. The raiders need not represent all goblins. The raid on the Creator's workshop still needs a credible motive; a paid job and a mistaken target remain possible, not decided.
- **[User]** The first boss can simply be a bear falling from the sky. Scout fights it, and it flees. Do not explain the fall. A later encounter may involve the same bear with a bell stuck on its head. On a return trip to Hobford, a third encounter may lead to a bear mount and a bear whistle.
- **[User]** Hunny Horror may live in Hobford's woods or a cave and may be involved in the later bear story. The actual relationship and encounter are unresolved. Keep the two bears distinct.
- **[User]** Returning with double jump should open routes visible but unreachable at first, including a climb above the Creator's mountain workshop and possibly passage over the mountain. The opening dimension can be much larger than level one.
- **[User]** Reserve a dramatic single-word boss title as a possible separate joke. The goose is undecided; the rolling-cheese incident was rejected.

## Gate travel and social consequences

- **[User]** Gate sickness is a strong, immediate deterrent. A typical biological traveler may need about a day to recover; Alice, Bob, Eve, and Mallory need roughly an hour. Tolerance may reflect both biology and practice. Robots are less affected. **No cumulative gate-sickness plot effects.**
- **[User]** Gates carry commerce, but most ordinary residents may avoid crossing. Distinct cultures persist despite trade. Animals and mounts also need recovery after crossing; this can support collecting and using different mounts in different dimensions, without real-time waiting.
- **[User]** Commonly used addresses cannot plausibly stay secret when travelers can watch the dial. Distinguish discovered addresses, published maps/directories, and unknown places. The GA can control terminals and information, not make public routes unknowable.
- **[User]** The Gate Authority needs check-ins, repairs, and contingency procedures for broken terminals. In the proposed opening, the bear fight damages Hobford's dialer so that only one useful onward connection remains selectable. Alice and Bob take that detour rather than wait for repairs. This restriction is a story/terminal idea, not an implemented gate-network rule.
- **[User]** R. Badly is stranded away from home after a terrible crossing. He wants to return but fears the gate more than anything, despite his bravado. Pirates can ride different *burning flying animals*; the fire makes them fly. His mount is a shark. Scout's bear might later gain burning flight, with sustained travel distinct from limited, precise boot jets.

## The provisional early route

- **[User]** The GA commissioned Scout for intended assignment to Alice. Alice did not personally commission Scout. Why the GA contracted the Creator rather than supplying a standard robot is still open. The Creator need not know any hidden strategic value.
- **[User]** Alice and Bob are recurring map characters, met throughout dimensions, not permanent escorts. They help with the first bear; they intend to return to their usual base in a major GA metropolis but must use the damaged dialer's detour.
- **[User]** The detour arrives at an *auxiliary gate terminal* in a techno-western industrial frontier dimension, not the largest city. Alice and Bob rest at its recovery inn and may give Scout money and directions to an ordinary local mechanic. Scout's autonomy should be established naturally, but the exact event is unresolved.
- **[User]** Scout may be knocked off a bridge onto a train during a robbery **committed by another train**. The outlaw locomotive steals cargo from trains for its own gain. The incident carries Scout far from Alice and Bob and opens a substantial independent adventure; it is not merely a sprint from the bear to another boss.
- **[User]** The frontier can mix *Cowboy Bebop*, *Firefly*, *Space Dandy*, *Red Dead Redemption*, and *StarCraft* Terran flavors: Western settlements, rails, industrial robotics, strange outlaws, and genuine adventures. The Galwah duel could fit here. A separate agent is working on a cowboy robot chicken boss (Foghorn Leghorn / DC Cyborg / cowboy hat); do not preempt or overwrite that work.
- **[User]** Later, a major encounter leaves Scout damaged enough to end up in a scrapyard. Fia finds Scout; Oiler repairs them. Oiler's expertise or shop may be part of why the frontier's larger city prospers. Alice and Bob eventually learn that their ordinary mechanic recommendation somehow led Scout to an exceptional mechanic. **The damaging boss, its motive, and the scrapyard transition are not yet chosen.**

## Character direction and later stops

- **[User]** Alice and Bob love exploring and mapping. Eve loves listening and collecting secrets; Mallory loves sabotage and destruction. All four are happy to be paid. Eve and Mallory should first be compelling villains without elaborate backstories. Eve sells intelligence to Nazi employers but is not necessarily a Nazi herself. Alice and Bob's maps are useful to those employers until their independent discoveries become a liability.
- **[User]** Alice and Bob's relationship is never confirmed or denied. "Allison" as Alice's full name, and perhaps Bob's first and last names both being Bob, are *background-only* wordplay suggested by speech-to-text; do not build a running joke around it.
- **[User]** Scout uses they/them by default. The Creator asks "Are you a boy or a girl?" with three randomly ordered answers: "I'm a boy," "I'm a girl," "I'm a robot." Do not explain the joke. A wearable bow is a separate cosmetic choice, independent of all three answers.
- **[User]** Math Town can come after Scout meets Fia and Oiler; Oiler may send Scout there on an errand. Move Hylbert's Hotel closer to the gate. Its numbered rooms could form a deterministic, addressable roguelike: room number as seed, arbitrary-number portal selection, and saved changes only for visited/modified rooms.
- **[User]** A Math Town visitor's luggage may use the optimal arrangement of 17 equal squares. A later optional packing puzzle can use the same arrangement as an implicit clue, without forcing every player to know the mathematics.
- **[User]** An intended main path is useful for finishing an end-to-end game. Alternate paths and outcomes can grow later. Character motivations and local incidents should stand on their own instead of all serving a single conspiracy.

## Suggestions not yet adopted

- **[Assistant]** A freight-handling machine, Scrap Knight, walking factory, and various sentient industrial objects were floated as second-boss candidates. None was selected. The outlaw train has stronger user support as an *adventure and transport event* than as the boss that wrecks Scout.
- **[Assistant]** A dedicated gate-recovery stable, mechanical wildlife, and other small frontier incidents remain optional. Do not treat them as required content.
