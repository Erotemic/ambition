# Character, gate, and player-agency ideas — November 22, 2026

**Status:** Compact brainstorm bank, not canon or a task list. `[User]` = proposed, clarified, or favored by the maintainer; `[Assistant]` = unadopted suggestion. An `[Open]` detail must not become a requirement merely because it appears here. This follows the [November 18–21 world directions](story_and_world_directions_2026-11-18.md) and [Hobford/frontier notes](2026-11-20-hobford-gates-and-frontier.md).

## Scout, Dennis, Alice, and Fia

- **[User]** **Dennis the Finch** is Scout's local maker, **not** the universe's creator. He may have a partner, **Mabel Finch**, helping make/raise Scout; a single maker versus a couple remains undecided. The loss of one or both in the opening would feel different, but do not add Mabel merely to increase the tragedy. The Gate Authority commissioned the ordinary robot for intended use by **Alice**. Scout is not secretly an exceptional machine; luck, upgrades, friends, and player actions carry the adventure.
- **[User-liked]** During the tutorial Dennis installs a **Protective Layer of Titanium**. A small `PLoT` marking is visible on the armor only if examined closely; nobody says "plot armor" or explains the joke. Afterward Dennis can ask how the new armor feels and mention that the lands beyond Hobford need mapping. The armor need not be a secret invincibility mechanism, and other equipment should remain equipable.
- **[User-proposed; strong direction]** Most robots have an assigned **objective module**; Scout may conspicuously lack one. They have no mandatory scripted ambition or owner-set goal. Alice is surprised that Scout does not request orders, and must **ask** for help. NPCs usually presume Scout is following some objective and ask what they are trying to do; the **player chooses the answer**. Do not invent a default personal desire for Scout that overrides player choice. Scout uses they/them by default.
- **[User-proposed]** Fia later discovers Scout damaged/out of commission in a scrapyard; **Oiler repairs** them. She could install an objective module but chooses not to. **[Open]** Why she abstains; whether objective-free robots are illegal; how Scout earlier helps Fia (if at all); and which larger encounter sends Scout to the scrapyard. Fia's decision need not be explained through a forced debt of gratitude or a scripted thesis. Her possible later betrayal arc remains open.

## Western: one train, outlaws, and proof assistants

- **[User]** The Western Dimension has a **working passenger-and-freight train** traversing towns and wilderness. In a 2D world the line can wrap horizontally so the train runs continuously without turning around. **[Open]** A true Möbius identification would reverse vertical orientation and is not required; an ordinary periodic loop solves the traversal problem. The *Rick and Morty* Story Train was an association, not a mandate to make the train a meta-narrative machine.
- **[User]** **Marshal Gallus “Rocq”** wants to rob the train **because he is an outlaw**. Scout gets in the way. No larger scheme or symbolic reason is needed. Rocq is a chicken; **[User-proposed]** he might be native to the Dinosaur Dimension and thus give Scout a personal reason to visit. He can travel through gates relatively easily.
- **[User-favored]** Keep **Peyote Lean** and Rocq as a duo if possible. A gang of outlaw proof assistants needs no elaborate rationale: **Isabelle/Isabel, Agatha (Agda), Daphne (Dafny), Iris, Pearl (NuPRL)** are possible members, not a locked cast. Peyote might be a dependable or truthful outlaw, riffing on Lean's trusted kernel; **[Open]** making Peyote the sheriff is an alternate, less favored configuration. Do not make each outlaw a walking software pun.

## Roo Badely and the consequences of crossing

- **[User]** Roo is **not native to PVN**. He can theoretically go home but cannot bring himself to cross another gate after a devastating reaction. Perhaps he was an early **Gate Authority recruit** before testing customs existed; a crossing left him incapacitated for something like **a year**, and he built a pirate life in the dimension where he was stranded. **[Open]** His origin may be the Central Hub; the precise service history and recovery timeline are provisional.
- **[User]** **Badely is a pirate title, not a uniquely numbered identity**. Roo succeeded a previous Badely through inheritance, mutiny, or another event still to be chosen. The pirates do not track the lineage like Zorlack's numbered Shadow Clan. Roo now raids shipments that arrive in PVN rather than crossing gates himself.
- **[User]** Biological gate sickness has a **wide distribution**. Most travelers are out of commission about a day; lucky travelers need roughly an hour; a minority take longer; Roo's case is exceptionally rare. No exact probability law is fixed. A visible **wormhole residue** clings to recent travelers. It dissipates almost at once from nonbiological beings; in biological travelers it persists during illness. Roo's lingering residue made the severity of his condition visible to other people.
- **[User-favored]** Greater **distance or angular separation between dimensions** likely increases severity, yet individual susceptibility remains important: Roo suffered terribly even though PVN and his possible home dimension are relatively near. Geometric dimensional distance obeys the **triangle inequality**; intermediate stops cannot shorten a route overall, but can allow recovery between crossings. **[Open]** Nonlinear severity and any same-dimension baseline reaction might make multi-hop trips or gate-tolerance rites useful. Avoid cumulative long-term gate-sickness plots by default.
- **[User]** **Emmy Ethereal is an exceptional biological traveler with zero apparent crossing effect.** Through symmetry/invariants and her aura, she maintains or reconstructs her state on the far side and may interact with gates outside their intended interface. She is a powerful **ally against the Nazis**, not an enemy. **[User-favored; later-game possibility]** Emmy might teach Roo how to cross safely, if he has the patience and willingness; her Noether-inspired habit of teaching students at home may inform the relationship. Her ability to protect *others* is not yet established.

## Gate power, trade, and History

- **[User]** The **Boss of the Game and his agents** are the only ones known to make reliable, high-quality real dimensional gates. Dangerous, unstable **bootleg gates** are possible. **Ownership** follows power/influence over a gate; **operation** follows practical ability and permission to use it. The GA imposes restrictions through **gate dial devices**, which materially expands its authority over a network it did not itself create. Keep this distinct from having created every gate.
- **[User]** Interdimensional knowledge spreads **through trade**, as it does over distance within a world; there are transit costs and bottlenecks. Technology isn't freely handed out by the GA, nor is its diffusion magically stopped.
- **[User]** **History is the most recently discovered dimension**. Its vast, freely invented geography has mountain ranges, seas, deserts, or other barriers separating distinctive civilizations and supporting relatively stable borders. **Not every civilization has a gate.** Those with gates gain commercial and political advantages; people absolutely adopt imported technology when they can afford and acquire it. Uneven contact, markets, and changing incentives produce internal conflict. **[Open]** How to make this enormous dimension playable without flattening real cultures or breaking Ambition's tone; a small initial region remains an option, not a promise.

## Worlds, gods, and supporting characters

- **[User]** The **Noodle Dimension** needs a **ramen bar**. Its apparent fit with Flying Spaghetti Monster worship is an **unexplained coincidence** and compelling evidence to believers inside the universe; inhabitants generally cannot invoke the out-of-world explanation that the game's author wanted a Noodle Dimension. Other dimensions have no comparably explicit central deity. **[User-proposed]** Girdle could have a proof that the FSM did **not create the universe**, without denying that it is a god. **[User-favored]** Scout should **actually kill the Flying Spaghetti Monster**, not merely defeat a projection, take a test, or survive its attacks. The god might be a large **stationary, wall-embedded boss** with appendages reaching into the room. **[Open]** Why Scout fights and kills it, and what follows.
- **[User]** **PCA (Perfect Cellular Automaton)** is pompous, showboaty, cultured, deadpan, witty, and thrill-seeking; comic-character energy reminiscent of Team Four Star's Cell (and to a lesser extent DevilArtemis' Cell). PCA **sincerely believes they are already perfect**; no insecurity, aspiration to become perfect, or need to prove the claim. The **Busy Beaver** belongs in Automaton; **[Assistant]** Bea, a small machine doing an immensely long finite construction project, is a possible interpretation, not an adopted name/design.
- **[User-liked; tentative]** **Mr. Sleight**, a mundane stage magician performing sleight of hand in the Magic Dimension, is an amusing possible resident. No commitment to include him.
- **[User-favored]** The existing **Patent Clerk**, an Einstein reference, stays **low-key** in Gnu-ton's Gravity Frame dispute. An offhand comment that Newton/Gnu-ton's theory (or Tesla's) is "quaint" can hint that the clerk recognizes deeper physics. He does not solve the lawsuit or take over the storyline. Whether to use Tesla's historical name rather than a parody is open.

## The apple pie, the endgame, and defeat

- **[User]** Keep three roles separate: **Dennis** built Scout; **the Boss of the Game** is the in-story being making an apple pie by following a cookbook whose first instruction is effectively *invent the universe*; **the game's human author** created Ambition. The Boss is not oblivious to the universe or Scout: he may take pleasure in what has emerged, and might regard Scout's appearance as part of his creation. **[Open]** Whether Scout was specifically intended or arose naturally. The GA knows of the final-fight Boss Dimension and **classifies its address** (recorded in the earlier note).
- **[User-proposed]** An optional **death/defeat count**, **corpse run**, or pre-fatal **portal rescue** could reconcile gameplay retries with Scout remaining alive. Another valid approach is to **reset to a checkpoint without an in-world time-rewind explanation**. A rewind *presentation* effect might replay a small recent frame/pose buffer rather than run expensive simulation rollback. **[Open]** Equipment recovery, multiplayer deaths, whether any key character deaths are persistent, and how much of failure/rewind is diegetic. Do not assign immortality or literal timeline control to PLoT armor solely to rationalize respawns.

**Still worth discovering:** Why Fia leaves Scout unassigned; what Scout and Alice choose to do together; whether Emmy can help Roo; why the FSM must die; what the Boss actually wants of Scout. These are **story questions**, not missing implementation requirements.
