# Characters, the Void, and an open-ended world — November 26–27, 2026

**Status:** Maintainer brainstorm, not a finalized plot or implementation queue. Distinguish favored directions from suggestions and open choices. This supplements the November 18–22 brainstorms, especially `2026-11-22-characters-travel-and-player-agency.md` and `the_fia_arc.md`. Where an older proposed arc conflicts with these newer directions, reconsider it rather than trying to preserve both literally.

## Develop characters before deciding their arcs

- **Favored:** Write character bibles rather than prescribing everyone's final state. Think of characters as having a derivative/evolution law: their personality, values, competencies, relationships, experience, and circumstances determine what they do next. Plot is the result of their interactions, not a destination that every scene must force them toward.
- Establish what each character enjoys, wants, notices, does well, overlooks, finds funny, and does under pressure. Include what can change their minds and how they behave differently with different people. A predetermined redemption or betrayal is not a required field.
- Use short exploratory scenes as experiments; they need not ship or become canon. Start with Scout, Fia, Alice, Bob, Roo Badely, the Boss, and PCA. Bob particularly needs development independent of his plot utility.
- Preserve Scout's player-directed agency and default they/them pronouns. Dennis the Finch built Scout, not the universe. Dennis dies in the introductory raid. Alice and Bob care deeply for each other; never put them on opposing sides, and never define their romantic status, including through dialogue that probes it.

## The Gate Authority and Delta Command

- **Favored direction:** Keep the Gate Authority as the larger organization administering the gate network, with **Delta Command** as its military arm. Exact jurisdiction, hierarchy, founding history, and terminology remain open. Do not rename every GA reference to Delta Command.
- The military has a legitimate and necessary protective role in a universe containing an actual expansionist Nazi regime. Acknowledge that necessity and lament the circumstances that make it necessary. Neither idealize war nor imply that disarmament in the face of a real threat is desirable.
- Within the same organization, some people genuinely want to protect others; some seek persecution, prestige, and power; some sincerely believe that persecution constitutes protection. Do not make the military branch uniformly good and civilian administration uniformly bad (or vice versa).
- Show the organization actually protecting civilians as well as abusing its authority over travel, access, trade and information. Reformers and critics should have agency of their own. Roo's earlier GA service is a possible avenue for personal connections and conflicting loyalties, not a new settled account of why he left.
- **Liked dialogue concept, draft wording:**

  > Scout: Are you proud of what Delta Command has accomplished?  
  > Commander: Very.  
  > Scout: Did you always want to be a soldier?  
  > Commander: No. I wanted to be an astronomer.  
  > Scout: Do you regret becoming a soldier?  
  > Commander: No. I regret that we needed another soldier more than we needed another astronomer.

  The commander need not advocate disbanding Delta Command, and can be decisive in defense. Their regret concerns the necessity, not the duty.

## Bob, the crypt, and a genuinely consequential rescue

- **New favored idea:** Scout finds Bob in a crypt/barrow during the cave adventure and can rescue him. Fit this into the existing early Hobford opening, including the topology discovery and later bear encounter; reconcile exact sequencing rather than assume an old cave/crypt layout is fixed.
- Bob gets a **cryptographer/cartographer** joke. Candidate line (workshop the delivery): "I suppose I'm less of a cartographer and more of a cryptographer these days." Another possibility: "I've been doing a little cryptography." Do not explain the pun.
- **Strong new possibility, not yet a committed branch:** Saving Bob is the intended/common path, but it should be genuinely possible not to save him and continue in a world where he dies. This is not a mandatory rescue disguised as a choice or a game-over condition. Alice's subsequent decisions could change dramatically because Bob is no longer there. Preserve her care for him without defining their relationship or reducing her to grief alone.
- Decide the circumstances, what the player can know/do, what makes the rescue succeed or fail, and how subsequent dialogue, relationships, discovery routes and scenes account for Bob's absence. Whether the failure is time-based, a conscious choice, or a consequence of other actions is open. Do not silently turn merely exploring at one's own pace into an irreversible failure. This should be explored as a real alternate world state, not implemented as a scripted death yet.
- **Bob characterization candidates:** Alice loves discovery; Bob enjoys understanding how things fit together, maps, topology, and possibly the structure of the gates. Give him interests, humor, initiative, and independent relationships rather than making him only Alice's cautious counterpart. Questions about who controls maps and gate addresses may provide situations to explore, not an obligatory political arc.

## Fia: negative space, subtraction, and possible tragedy

- **Favored:** Fia is particularly competent at understanding absence, omission, and removal. Early Fia communicates through what is not said; she is comfortable with silence and can infer what others leave implicit. Show her practical skill at removing unwanted things without damaging what should remain. Foreshadow the Void through ordinary repairs, not ominous prophecy.
- **Oiler dialogue seed:** "Best rust remover I've ever worked with. Gets all the rust off without taking any of the good metal." Other repair examples: removing noise, unnecessary parts, corrosion, and obstructions. Let these read as genuine competence on a first playthrough.
- **Preserve the darker applications of those same qualities:** misleading without technically lying; shaping conclusions by withholding information; exploiting the distinction between agreement and lack of explicit refusal; treating absence of opposition as consent.
- The Void form looks great; retain it. Acquiring the form need not replace Fia's personality or constitute supernatural moral corruption. Keep the established black-and-white Void motif.
- **Open:** Redemption. The maintainer likes Fia enough to want her redeemed, but withholding redemption may be more powerful, especially given the existing name-related payoff. Cultivate genuine hope through a character worth caring about, real opportunities to turn back, and recognizable affection/competence even late in the story. If she refuses, the player should feel the loss of that possibility. Do not commit to this outcome until character development supports it.
- Alternatives considered: accepting responsibility and repairing damage after going too far; refusing a catastrophic plan; acting as a collaborator or independent saboteur rather than a ruler. Avoid mechanically recreating Kerrigan by making Fia the leader of a Void army and a third geopolitical faction. No need to invent a worse replacement villain just to make her redeemable. The new-antagonist idea remains optional.

## The Void and the Creator

- **Favored:** The Void need not be malevolent. Ambition is intentionally maximalist, which also creates clutter and redundancy. The Void can be a natural subtractive process that tends to restore negative space and balance, analogous to osmosis/concentration gradients (an analogy, not a fixed physical law). It does not need to judge moral worth. Feature Creep already exists; do not explain that connection as a joke or lecture.
- Fia's choices about what to remove can differ from the Void's natural behavior. Its power is not itself an excuse for her choices.
- **Replace the older Boss motivation of wanting a challenge/worthy opponent.** The Boss is very non-serious. He often creates things because they seemed cool, or because he liked them. He can enjoy a challenge and get intensely focused on it without having high emotional stakes in winning. Keep the apple-pie-from-scratch purpose and the universe-as-work-in-progress premise.
- The Boss may have made the Void because he knows his own tendency to add too much. A system that counteracts his own preferences can communicate intellectual humility without a lecture about bias or pride. Draft exchange: "I kept putting things in. Figured something ought to take things out." / "Couldn't you have just stopped putting things in?" / "I could have."
- Preserve the game's tension between flippancy and seriousness. The Creator's low stakes or silly reasons for creating something do not make the inhabitants' lives and suffering insignificant. Let some scenes be funny, and others genuinely serious without interrupting them with jokes. Don't explain jokes, themes, or moral lessons unnecessarily.

## Build the beginning-to-ending spine now

- The Boss Dimension is an actual destination. A player who knows its gate address and can access a gate can reach it **at any time**; do not add obligatory boss counts or quest flags to enforce a narrative trajectory. The GA knows and classifies the address.
- Establish a playable path from the rural Hobford opening to gate access, a reason to seek this address, a discoverable route to learning it, entry into the boss chamber, an encounter, and an ending. The exact reason and discovery route remain open; respect Scout's player-directed motivations. A known-address shortcut is intended freedom, not a sequence break to patch out.
- Establish the convention early that entering a boss chamber initiates a boss fight. The participants need not hate one another. The bear can establish the first-boss convention in Hobford; write the actual incident and its aftermath. Defeat need not mean killing. The recurring bear and later mount payoff are possibilities already discussed, not requirements for every route.
- The Boss can simply start a fight when Scout arrives; it need not be the logical culmination of every other story. Post-fight interactions may depend on whom Scout met or lost, but unresolved optional stories should not prevent an ending. Retain the current direction of depleting resolve and voluntary retirement, with the final conversation to be developed.
- Once that spine works, stuff the middle with independent content. Character bibles and local stories can develop in parallel without requiring each to serve the final quest.

## A populous world, dungeons, and distributed bosses

- **Favored:** Populate the world heavily. Take inspiration from Hollow Knight's intricate backgrounds and simple, readable character silhouettes, not its minimal population. Busy towns, independent NPC activity, varied wilderness, quiet spaces when useful, and readable combat. Negative space gains meaning from contrast; a suddenly empty familiar town may be much more striking than an always-sparse world.
- Aim for many distributed bosses (roughly forty is an illustrative scale, not a quota). Boss fights can exist because they are fun, funny, mechanically interesting, or appropriate to a location. They need not all be cosmologically important or related to one another.
- Dungeons are a major content unit. Develop the barrows (including smaller optional burial chambers), the Gram Reaper dungeon / Hilbert Hotel spectral wing, and many more. Other cave, mine, ruin and fortress ideas remain optional. Do not impose a single dungeon formula or narrative justification.
- The new **Barrow Wight** is an ancient armored burial guard with a grave-marker maul, an overhead windup, and a recovery window. It is intended to replace the Nazi/Risen Conscript stand-ins in the barrow passage. A separate sprite/catalog/map overlay exists; verify application and playtest before treating its exact stats or placement as shipped.
- Math Town gag: **The Reader**, a book-reading resident exercising (dumbbell curls), for "left as an exercise to the reader." A separate sprite overlay exists. Place him in Math Town when developing the town; no explanatory dialogue required.
