# Creator, Agents, and Endgame — Candidate Story Directions

**Status: non-canonical brainstorm.** This records possibilities discussed in conversation, not approved lore, required features, or commitments to an ending. The options below can coexist, conflict, or be discarded. Names and proposed character traits are provisional unless already established elsewhere in the repository.

## Grounding: creator intent (direct quotes)

> “I think I want myself to be the boss of the game, that's a name I've gone by. and it can play into the god/creator theme, eg fsm”

> “i view them as my enforcers sometimes advisors or aids in game.”

> “I think the trick is going to remove the creators access to an Llm or give him a small model. although I'll still have fight in me without my agent. I just change the world slower.”

> “I think I ultimately aquiess. I'm not defeated. I sought a thrill and got it. I never sought to be the villain, merely be challenged.”

> “I'm interested in role playing and occasionally that means ruthlessness.”

> “I'm not sure I want to go in the lawful evil direction.”

> “the bossofthegame is the bossofthegame until the day he dies, or retires”

The Boss is an in-world role, **not a declaration of the real creator's morality**. Different alignments and degrees of ruthlessness are material to explore, not preselected traits.

## Candidate overarching plots

- **Stolen thunder / price of light:** Nikobolt and Coulomba Tesseract investigate stolen wireless-power designs, Castle Godwin's lightning, and possible exploitation by an Industrial Dimension. Electricity genuinely improves lives while potentially exporting environmental and social costs to another world. Resolving the conflict requires engineering, negotiation, and accountability—not merely killing a villain.
- **The missing worlds / last cartographer:** Gate addresses, old records, and messages reveal dimensions that have been disconnected or forgotten. Nikobolt investigates the mechanism; Coulomba investigates what happened to the people. Who should reopen the gates, and on what terms?
- **The great exhibition:** Industrial, Preindustrial, Art, and other worlds contest the meaning and ownership of progress. Art, journalism, and public testimony can build understanding or become propaganda. Multiple communities have legitimate but conflicting interests.
- **The unfinished world:** Abandoned concepts, earlier character incarnations, removed regions, or discarded worlds survive in the Void. Their residents may *not* want restoration. The creator's development history becomes potential mythology, without requiring players to know software development.
- **Distributed, self-directed stories:** Each dimension has local politics, humor, faiths, relationships, and ordinary life. The Boss need not secretly cause every problem, and no single plot must absorb all the world's stories.

## The Boss: authority, ennui, and moral tension

- The Boss is the creator/developer inside the fiction, able to change mechanics and worlds, but not necessarily omniscient, omnipotent, infallible, wise, or benevolent. He seeks novelty and a **worthy challenge**; boredom or deeper **ennui** may motivate interventions.
- **Variable ethical conduct:** protective, playful, detached, authoritarian, principled, reckless, or ruthless in different circumstances. One possible excursion explores **lawful evil**: perfectly enforced procedures that preserve the Boss's unilateral authority. This is an option, **not the chosen alignment**.
- **Optional Saitō-inspired direction:** an uncompromising personal code and willingness to be ruthless, inspired by Saitō from *Rurouni Kenshin*, without treating him—or the Boss—as necessarily lawful evil. The middle-school “bossofthegame” joke echoes the series' “manslayer” line; it is a personal reference, not established game canon. Uncompromising convictions and disagreements with protagonists may be more useful than a fixed good/evil grid. Intent, consequences, and legitimacy can remain in tension; the Boss need not end with a redemption or sermon.
- **Impermanence:** places evolve, relationships change, creations become obsolete, people are remembered differently, and attempts to preserve everything can become a kind of control. Ordinary lives continue independently of the Boss and player.

## Agents as characters and temple powers

- GPT-, Claude-, and Qwen-inspired **parody characters** serve the Boss as **enforcers, advisors, and aids**, with different assignments and potentially conflicting advice. **Gepetto** is the GPT-inspired agent's proposed self-chosen name; other names and appearances remain open.
- Low/high/extra-high thinking settings, different models, multiple instances, server farms, local fallbacks, tooling, and parallel subagents can translate into gameplay. The **extra-high** configuration might be the temple boss while smaller instances appear as helpful NPCs.
- Distinctive temples could test **construction and rules** (Gepetto), **interpretation, records, and delegation** (Claude counterpart), or **fabrication and operations** (Qwen counterpart). These are *fictional specializations*, not claims about real models.
- Disabling a server farm reduces throughput, coordination, or tool access. Existing creations remain; isolated agents may keep following old instructions. Temples might be hostile defenses, authorized tests, or ambiguous encounters rather than automatic death matches.

## Religious and philosophical candidates

- **Creator versus God:** proof that the Boss built the world does not establish moral authority, ultimate metaphysical status, or a right to worship. A maker may be a *demiurge*, dependent on logic, hardware, and rules he did not invent.
- **Problem of evil / divine command:** who suffers when the creator wants a compelling challenge? Is a rule good because the creator decreed it? How can inhabitants appeal decisions when the Boss controls the appeals process?
- **Flying Spaghetti Monster / Noodly Temple:** theological satire, conflicting revelation, and unfalsifiable claims—without depicting all believers as foolish. Religions might also supply real solidarity, mutual aid, and institutions even when metaphysical claims conflict.
- **Impermanence, ennui, and attachment:** Buddhist-inspired questions about change and unsatisfactoriness; a creator who can make anything yet cannot guarantee lasting satisfaction. Preservation, immortality, archival memory, and the right to change or leave can conflict.
- **Open source as metaphysics:** a player can challenge the creator *within* the game, but changing the ultimate rules requires the source; a fork raises questions about freedom, ownership, and becoming a creator oneself.

## Candidate endgame, with alternatives

Players learn how the agents and world-changing infrastructure work, then restrict remote models, subagents, and local compute. The Boss **changes the world more slowly**, but still fights and uses existing machinery and skill. The encounter can draw on mechanics from across Ambition rather than simply raising enemy health.

One ending: the Boss **acquiesces rather than dies**. He enjoyed being challenged and did not program a permanent defeat condition. The long-running refrain, **“the bossofthegame is the bossofthegame until the day he dies, or retires,”** can become a recurring joke and literal rule: **the only definitive in-game victory is getting the Boss to retire voluntarily** (not depleting a health bar). Alternatively, someone can change the rules in a fork of the open-source game. The public repository offers an optional invitation to implement another ending; the shipped game should still end satisfactorily. The Boss might be gracious, irritated, dangerous, morally ambiguous, or still authoritarian afterward. **His acquiescence need not mean moral conversion or relinquishing authority.**

**Still open:** What actually motivates the player to confront the Boss? Which costs of world-editing persist? Are the agents loyal, conflicted, testing the player, or varying by instance? How much of this is comedy, political drama, theological satire, or existential reflection? Which candidate plots become connected, and which remain independent?
