# Story and world directions — November 18, 2026

**Status:** Brainstorm record, not a finalized plot or canon rewrite. These are decisions and strong preferences from a creative discussion, plus explicitly unresolved possibilities. Preserve competing ideas for later curation; do not automatically promote every suggestion into canon.

## Narrative approach

- **Go big.** Ambition should contain substantial, independently interesting characters, dimensions, and adventures. Not every plot needs to connect to the GA, Fia, or a single final conflict; intersections can emerge later.
- **Branch meaningfully.** LLM-supported stories should sustain many more persistent character trajectories and consequences than a conventional fixed dialogue tree. Maintain a comprehensible backbone of major events without resetting relationships or forcing all paths to become identical.
- **Use a story bible.** Character pages and world sheets should provide motivations, history, relationships, institutions, and creative affordances; playthrough state should separately record what actually happened and who knows it. The bible is a writer's resource, not necessarily player-facing exposition.
- **Explore before committing.** Conversations are an idea-mining space. Keep provisional possibilities distinct from settled directions, and don't force premature coherence.

## Opening, Alice and Bob, Fia

- **Revised opening:** Scout spends the Hobford opening with the Creator, Alice, and Bob. Fia and Oiler **do not** repair Scout immediately after the lab escape. Scout first goes through a gate, survives a boss encounter, and sustains damage; the later repair brings Scout to Oiler and Fia. This supersedes the *immediate workbench* opening proposed in `the_fia_arc.md` and early Oiler-at-the-lab-exit brainstorms.
- **Alice's vocation:** She recognizes the GA's flaws yet sincerely values the work she and Bob do as cartographers. Preserve the spirit of: “I know the GA isn't the greatest, but we do good work out here. People need maps. Somebody has to get them from A to B.” Do not append an explanatory moral speech.
- **Alice's political branch is later**, well after the first gate expedition. She may stay with the GA, become neutral or independently helpful, or eventually join a principled resistance and lose her standing with the GA. Her commitment is not a dialogue check that Scout resolves in Hobford.
- **Alice and Bob are a durable pair.** They show deep mutual care and loyalty; they can disagree or differ in their level of political involvement, but never end up on opposing sides. **Do not establish or deny a romantic relationship.** The player is never offered the question, and the game should not contrive an interruption, explanation, or joke about the ambiguity. This supersedes older proposals for an explicit romance branch.
- **Fia** remains an intelligent, ambitious mechanic working under Oiler, with a potential friendship and betrayal arc. She is **more Machiavellian than Alice**. An extreme branch may bring her into alliance with Nazis because of her hatred of the GA; this is neither inevitable nor an endorsement of that alliance. Her motives, intermediary choices, and eventual outcomes still need development.

## The Gate Authority and its opponents

- The **GA merits both loyalty and resistance**. It maintains useful interdimensional infrastructure and **genuinely fights Nazis**. It is also seriously afflicted by corruption, rigid hierarchy, institutional self-protection, and excessive deference to tradition. Some of its authoritarian mechanisms resemble those of its enemies; that does **not** erase consequential differences, including the Nazis' genocidal commitments.
- Characters may argue that the GA and the Nazis are “all the same.” Explore why that claim feels persuasive to people harmed by the GA, where the comparison is apt, and where it fails. Some critics are principled; others exploit the comparison to excuse Nazi collaboration.
- **“The GA agenda”** is language used **only by paranoid, pro-Nazi agitators**, not by the GA, Alice, ordinary critics, or neutral narration. The satire targets people so terrified of a supposed cultural/“gay agenda” that they support fascists. The use is reclaimative. The game's framing will not affirm their conspiracy. The actual GA has redeeming qualities as well as serious abuses.

## The Void and the magical world

- **The Void is not simply evil or an automatic corrupter.** Mere contact does not mean somebody loses part of themselves. The Void lizard drinking tea on a bridge is compatible with this; keep the absurd image.
- A **deliberate, engineered Void interaction** might subtract something different from each person. For Fia, explore the loss of a *particular kind of moral self-questioning*: the ability to seriously entertain that she should relinquish control or abandon the goal. She must retain engineering skepticism, the capacity to detect errors, and practical reasoning. **The exact subtraction and whether it occurs are undecided.** The Void should not become a blanket excuse for her moral choices.
- The **Mary-O/magical dimension might be recently connected**, contrasting with engineering-based worlds through capabilities they cannot readily reproduce. Its age of contact, Mary’s relationship with the GA, and the nature of magic remain open questions.

## Pirate–ninja dimension

- **Make Roo and the ninja leader larger than life:** exceptional, formidable figures with enormous ambitions, not merely comic side characters.
- **Captain R. Badly** is the pirate captain; the **R stands for Roo**, a name the player may discover in private correspondence or a diary. Do not explain the name's layers in game dialogue. Roo sails, carouses, raids valuable GA shipments, and may dream of reaching the Noodle Dimension after hearing of its **soda volcano**.
- The pirates and ninjas **were enemies long ago but are now at peace**. Their present-day encounters are a largely professional rivalry, not a blood feud. The GA may have arrived approximately **20 years ago** (tentative), bringing lucrative cargo. Roo raids it; the GA hires the ninja clan to protect it. Roo and the ninja leader can respect each other: piracy is business, and ninjas need to earn a living too.
- **Zorlack 65536** (formerly spelled Xorlack in some notes) is the Shadow Clan leader and the last in his numbered succession. He respects the clan's history and his ceremonial role even as he tires of it. Earlier talk of a lifelong bureaucratic ambition has shifted toward **discovering what he actually wants**, possibly through fascination with the Business Dimension. See the later Zorlack notes below.
- The **ninja lair has a pond of turtles wearing ninja eye masks**. The TMNT allusion should stand as an environmental detail, without explanation or a reaction scene.
- *How to Kill a Mockingbird* remains a deliberate inspiration. A **giant Mockingbird can be an antagonist**, and **burning flying sharks** are a desired spectacle, with Ambition's own designs. **We are scrapping the moon-kill ending as the planned plot.**; the ancient pirate–ninja exploits may be part of this world's legendary past.

## References, tone, and Geppetto

- Ambition's creative identity includes **curating, blending, and transforming references** alongside original work, sometimes with the collision-of-worlds energy of *Robot Chicken*. The gate inscription **LEGALLY DISTINCT** is part of this sensibility. The reference catalog may spell out allusions **for writers**; the game should normally let them stand without explanatory dialogue or added jokes that spoil the effect.
- **Geppetto is a promising recurring character** worth developing: a GPT-inspired orchestrator associated with the Boss. Further traits, ambitions, and story arcs discussed for him were proposals, **not decisions to lock down**.

## Further directions — November 21, 2026

**Status key:** `[User-confirmed]` records an explicit decision; `[User-favored]` records an idea the maintainer liked, but not necessarily final implementation; `[Assistant-proposed]` remains optional. These notes capture a discussion, not a complete specification.

### Hylbert's Hotel: the spectral gap

- **[User-favored]** Hylbert used inductive magic to keep adding rooms for guests arriving through the gates. He showed that there was room for one more guest, but did not ensure that the hotel's structure remained uniformly stable as it grew. Small disturbances began to produce large and unpredictable effects; pillars shifted direction, and the hotel became haunted.
- **[User-favored]** The **spectral gap keeps the haunting at bay**. Scout can restore portal connections to increase the gap in a protected region and push the haunted frontier deeper into the hotel. Keep this mathematical idea central to the location. Transfinite cardinals do not need to explain the haunting.
- **[Mathematical grounding]** For a corridor of `n` rooms, the path-graph Laplacian gap is `2 - 2 cos(pi/n)`, which tends to zero as the corridor grows. A large, finite clique at the entrance cannot remove the low-energy modes of an infinite corridor beyond it. A connected infinite 3-regular tree, by contrast, has positive bottom of the `l^2` graph-Laplacian spectrum: `3 - 2 sqrt(2)`. This infinite-graph quantity is not the same as the second eigenvalue of a finite truncation.
- **[Assistant-proposed gameplay]** Portal puzzles rewire selected clusters and improve their finite-graph spectral gaps. A later repair changes the *generating rule* for room connections so the active guest network remains infinite but has a positive spectral bottom. The old **Spectral Wing** can remain quarantined, far from ordinary guests; its own infinite corridor can still be unstable. A finite number of new doors near the lobby alone cannot repair an infinite unstable tail.
- **[Precision]** A small structural spectral gap makes some disturbances easy to amplify. Large changes in the directions of individual eigenvectors also require near-degenerate eigenvalues; those are related but distinct phenomena. The haunting can reflect both, without claiming one gap proves everything.
- **[Open]** Why the Gram Reaper is haunting the hotel is unresolved. He need not have caused the instability. Do **not** make him Hylbert's hired auditor by default.

### Math Town and the wider Math Dimension

- **[User-favored]** Math Town is a settlement with ordinary civic life, not a neighborhood for every mathematical subject. The wider **Math Dimension** can have distinct regions, cultures, and landscapes influenced by mathematics. Proposed regions include the **Surreal Lands**, **Complex Plane**, **Topological Wilds**, **Fractal Frontier**, and **Non-Euclidean Highlands**. Their borders and relationships are open.
- **[User-favored]** A literal **Hackenbush** grows in the Math Dimension, perhaps in the Surreal Lands: red, blue, and green branches form a playable combinatorial game. When a cut disconnects branches from the ground, the disconnected structure vanishes. It can support a garden encounter, a competitive game, or traversal puzzles. Game values and surreal numbers may become part of local life.
- **[User-confirmed]** There will be **bird-headed religious cardinals**, including an **Inaccessible Cardinal**. **[User-favored]** A wooden **Woodin Cardinal** is another possibility; his existence might be a matter of dispute. These figures do not need to appear in Hylbert's Hotel. Cardinal hierarchy and theology remain to be invented.

### Governments, capitals, and voting

- **[User-confirmed]** **Castle Godwin is not the capital of the Nazi Dimension.** Most inhabited dimensions should have a capital or an equivalent seat of government. The Nazi capital's name and character remain open.
- **[User-favored]** The Nazi regime's **Supreme Leader** inherited the Reich. Explore a successor shaped by the previous regime's defeat or stalemate, with his own strategy and rival officials. His name, personal history, and exact power structure remain undecided; Wolfenstein is not this leader.
- **[User-favored]** Voting should recur in the game. Gate Authority Council elections and votes can have genuine consequences and difficult questions of representation. Elections in the Nazi Dimension can be staged or manipulated. Other dimensions may use their own electoral customs.
- **[User-favored]** Voting is also a mathematics mechanic: compare **plurality/FPTP**, **IRV (RCV)**, **approval**, **STAR**, and possibly **Condorcet** rules. The same preference profile can produce different outcomes under different methods; not all methods accept the same ballot information. Let players discover these effects through examples and choices.
- **[User-favored; presentation open]** A precise **11,780** allusion may appear in the electoral story. Its purpose is to distinguish counting votes from demanding a predetermined outcome. Keep this reference restrained for now; no specific dialogue, analogy, or placement has been approved. A theme worth retaining: **“Institutions must be accountable to reality, not merely to the people who hold power.”**

### Creator, Science Dimension, and project purpose

- **[User-confirmed canon in conversation]** The Creator made the universe because he was **trying to make an apple pie from scratch**, not because he wanted to be a villain or sought a worthy challenge. **The apple pie is far in the future: he has not finished the first step, inventing the universe.** This supersedes the creator-as-bored-challenger *motivation* in older proposals. Keep the eventual reveal short; do not explain the joke through extra dialogue. The verbatim-only `docs/storylines/cannon.md` is unchanged by this brainstorm record.
- **[User-favored; newer direction]** GNU-ton built a world using controllable **Gravity Frames** and tries to enforce sweeping patent claims against Nikobolt's independently developed alternative, including by force. His discovery and engineering remain real achievements; the conflict concerns his attempt to monopolize the idea. His orchards might connect to the Creator's future apple pie, but that is conjecture. See [`2026-11-21-gnu-ton-gravity-frame-patent-story.md`](2026-11-21-gnu-ton-gravity-frame-patent-story.md). Science Dimension institutions such as Eureka and the orbital station can still have independent stories.
- **[User-favored mission direction]** Ambition should combine joy and serious choices: robots, mathematics, pirates, ninjas, strange adventures, and moments when doing the right thing is hard. People need not face those moments alone. Support **couch and remote multiplayer**, an **open-source** world that others can extend, and **clear code and APIs** that make creation practical, not merely permitted.
- **[User-favored mission direction]** Be open that AI helps create the game. Aim to show what people can make with AI while recognizing the displacement and uncertainty faced by artists and coders. Estimate and publicly track development's environmental costs, with the limits of those estimates stated plainly. The game's commitment to evidence and accountability belongs here too, without making every adventure deliver a lesson.
- **[Open]** Stone golem warriors were floated as a possible addition, not an established faction or character.

## Additional world and character directions — November 21, 2026

### Seventeen-dimensional atlas and the Gate Authority

- **[User-confirmed roster]** Current broad world list: **Fantasy/Home, Pirate–Ninja (PVN), Central Hub, History, Nazi, Dinosaur, Western, Art, Science, Math, Magic, Noodle, Unfinished, Automaton, Neural, Boss, and Business**. These are creative destinations, not a claim that 17 complete worlds are already implemented. The **Business Dimension** fills slot 17. **Ocean** remains a possible future idea, **not** on this list: cross-gate fluid simulation would be a substantial technical undertaking.
- **[User-confirmed]** The **Boss Dimension** is specifically the destination for the game's **final boss fight**, not a world of assorted boss fights. A **Hall of Bosses** could also be there, but its placement and access are open. **[User-favored]** The GA **knows of this dimension and classifies its address**; it is not simply undiscovered. Who authorized classification, why, and how much they know remain mysteries. It need not appear on the ordinary public fast-travel atlas.
- **[User-favored]** The **Central Hub** is a major inhabited technical/civic capital and the center of the Gate Authority's military and administration, not just a room containing gates. Its population includes ordinary residents. The military can have recruits from elsewhere. **[User-confirmed]** The Nazi Dimension's capital is not Castle Godwin.
- **[User-favored]** Balance settled, character-rich places with combat/exploration-heavy worlds. Math, Science, Western, Business, and Central Hub lean toward inhabited institutions; Dinosaur, Noodle, Neural, Automaton and others can emphasize encounters while still containing societies. Not every world needs an equally expansive city.
- **[Open mechanics]** A broad fast-travel atlas might be a **2D projection of high-dimensional features/distances** (UMAP was discussed), not a literal map of political alliances or guaranteed direct gate routes. An alternative gate-travel rule is that **biological crossing effects increase with dimensional distance**; effects might instead be roughly uniform, or depend on compatibility/exposure. Narrative consequences and whether intermediate stops help are deliberately undecided.

### Business Dimension: everyone is doing business

- **[User-confirmed direction]** A maximalist world of **businessmen doing business**, Business College, business trips and flights to important meetings, handshakes, dead-serious professionals, office drama, TPS reports, deadlines, ledgers, executive summaries, public relations, corporate hierarchies and nonsense. Also include the real consequences: **worker exploitation, unionization, and cold cruelty justified as “only business.”** Satirize the institutions and behaviors, not every worker or business as inherently bad.
- **[User-favored]** It becomes important to interdimensional commerce, finance, contracts and politics *through its culture and institutions*, rather than being a generic marketplace with an economic label. A corporation and a local shop can both exist here.
- **[User-favored]** **Intellectual property goes to extremes.** Directions, maps and route information may require licensing or payment. **Alice and Bob could be arrested for freely sharing cartography**; the precise law, incident and consequences are not fixed. This connects naturally to GNU-ton's attempted gravity patent monopoly and the recurring `LEGALLY DISTINCT` motif.
- **[User-proposed; open]** Perhaps **patent law is ultimately settled by trial by combat**. A fully serious courtroom, legal briefs, attorneys and a physical fight make a possible setting or justification for GNU-ton's boss encounter. **[Assistant-proposed]** A patent appeal arena or “Patent Trial and Appeal Brawl” is a possible joke, not an adopted institution. Winning a fight should not be treated as settling the moral question of ownership.
- **[Assistant-proposed]** Some business metaphors could be literal level designs (corporate ladder, hostile takeover, restructuring), but no new simulation system is required merely to make the satire work.

### Zorlack 65536 and the end of a succession

- **[User-confirmed]** Use **Zorlack 65536**, not Xorlack; these refer to the same character. He **deeply respects and enjoys his clan's history and ceremonial garments**, though he is becoming tired of the life prescribed for him. He can put the garb aside; the point is his evolving relationship to a meaningful inheritance, not refusal to change clothes.
- **[User-favored]** The Shadow Clan has an ancestral **stone wall with a finite number of places for Zorlacks**, and Zorlack 65536 fills the **last** one. The clan has long anticipated that its numbered succession would end and its culture or way of governing would transition. There may be a prophecy or an inherited agreement: the last Zorlack **must lead until they discover what they truly love**. Exactly how the transition works, and whether it is prophecy, are open. **[Assistant-proposed]** A 256-by-256 grid would make the number legible.
- **[User-favored]** Zorlack may not know what he wants until his **first trip to the Business Dimension**. He is fascinated by the possibility of choosing a career, but cannot easily get an interview or satisfy corporate expectations. **[User-liked]** A company dress code demanding that he downplay his ceremonial identity is a character conflict, especially beside corporate claims to celebrate individuality. Do not stack ninja/Asian cultural stereotypes merely for comedy; the institutional absurdity belongs to the Business Dimension.
- **[Assistant-proposed]** Rejections despite real military leadership experience, or enrollment in Business College, are possible steps; neither his eventual vocation nor the clan's political future is decided. Roo's difficulty with gate travel need not apply to Zorlack.

### Gnu-ton, Coulomba, apples, and nonfatal defeat

- **[User-favored]** Keep **Gnu-ton's redemption arc**: he need not die. After a boss fight he can **stand down**, or at most be briefly **unconscious and recover**. Avoid a sentimental resurrection scene. **GNU1**, not GNU2, is the one associated with the *existing* golden-apple revival beat; this beat is now **disfavored**, not a story commitment. Defeat should precede a longer process of withdrawing coercion, repairing harm and learning to share.
- **[User-favored]** Gnu-ton **grows apples**; perhaps the orchard ultimately supplies the Creator's planned apple pie. This is a suggested link, **not** an established explanation for the orchard, apples, or golden-apple mechanics. The Creator has not finished creating the universe, let alone baking the pie.
- **[User-favored]** **Coulomba** was Gnu-ton's **former collaborator and lover**. She might specialize in communication/message magic and its networks, with the details of her role in the gravity dispute still open. Nikobolt Tesseract's name may change. **[User-favored mechanics]** Message magic includes **Dispatch** and **Attunement**, with **Signal** (wave-like attack) and **Responder** (timed counter) as techniques. Alice, Bob, Eve and Mallory can embody sending, attuning, eavesdropping and interference, respectively; exact implementations remain exploratory.
