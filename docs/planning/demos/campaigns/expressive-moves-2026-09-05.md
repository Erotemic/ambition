## What the campaign established

The campaign's durable result was not a fixed list of twelve bespoke move types.
It demonstrated that expressive platform-fighter moves should be composed from a
small set of reusable authorities:

- authored move/effect vocabulary;
- deterministic target arbitration;
- control/custody claims;
- delayed and world-anchored effects with separate attribution/geometry;
- persistent/triggered world objects;
- locomotion and traversal state;
- charge/meter/resource policy;
- body-owned presentation facts;
- rollback/session lifetime declared by the owning capability.

Several moves that initially looked like engine gaps turned out to be existing
capabilities with no authored customer. That is why current work starts from the
capability and parity inventories rather than from a new campaign list.

## Durable architecture outcomes

Work during the campaign contributed or exercised, among other things:

- counters/ripostes without a dedicated `CounterMove` type;
- controllable/delayed projectiles and stable target arbitration;
- mount/custody and possession interaction;
- temporary locomotion/control modes;
- mines/bombs/traps and environment-side damage;
- Limit/resource spending and fill sources;
- delayed body marks;
- portal recovery and portal presentation policy;
- tether/reel mechanics;
- reusable presentation facts rather than ruleset-aware rendering.

The campaign also produced several reusable lessons now encoded elsewhere:

1. geometry origin, damage eligibility and attack attribution are separate facts;
2. a capability that is compiled but never installed/customered is not accepted;
3. query/entity order is not arbitration;
4. a move whose mechanic is unreadable still lacks part of its implementation;
5. reusable capability mechanisms belong below game-specific balance/policy.
