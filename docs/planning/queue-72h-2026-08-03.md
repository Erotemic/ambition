- ▢ **B13. A BLOCK CANNOT NAME ITS OWN ART, and that is what blocks D11's texture,
  the interrobang, and every "this one looks different" a game will ever want.**
  `spawn_block` resolves art from `BlockKind` alone —
  `block_tile_sprite(Solid) → EntitySprite::SolidTile` — so **every solid block in
  a room shares one sprite** and no game can distinguish one from its neighbour.
  ⭐ **the precedent is already in the same struct.** `Block::art_color` is an
  authored, presentation-only, per-block hint the renderer reads BEFORE the
  kind-based path, and its doc states the gap it closed: *"until now content had no
  way to say 'this shape has no art yet'"*. **The seam needed is the positive form
  of exactly that** — `art_color` says *draw nothing*; this says *draw THIS*.
  ⭐ **and the shape is already proven twice for other entity classes**:
  `ProjectileVisualId` and `WorldItemArt` are OPEN STRING ids, registered by
  content and resolved by the render layer through a catalog. A block wants the
  same: `Block::art_sprite: Option<String>`, resolved through a block-art registry,
  **losing to `art_color`** (an explicit "no art" outranks a positive claim) and
  **beating `block_tile_sprite(kind)`**.
  ⚠ **it must be a STRING, not an `EntitySprite`**: that enum lives in the render
  crate and `ae::Block` is in core — naming it would invert the dependency. The
  string id is what keeps the layering honest, which is the same reason the
  projectile and world-item paths use one.
  ✔ **the handle already exists**: `BlockVisual` carries the block's `GeoId` as of
  the flinch work, so the render side can key per-block facts without inventing an
  identity.
  ⚠ **written as a row rather than built**, deliberately: it is a multi-crate seam
  (core field, registry, render resolver, content registration) and half of one is
  worse than none.
  ⭐⭐ **AND THERE IS A SECOND ANSWER, found by asking why the tileset was
  unconsumed — it is not a lost binding.** A generated tileset becomes live by
  being referenced from an `.ldtk` file: `intro.ldtk` names
  `intro_lab_tileset.png`, `town_tileset` likewise, and their PNGs sit in the asset
  tree. `super_mary_o_tileset` has neither, because **Mary-O's level 1-1 is
  CODE-authored** (`level_1_1`, `POWER_BLOCK_COLUMNS`, `power_block_min(i)`) and
  there is no `.ldtk` to reference it from. Nothing was dropped; there was never a
  level to bind it to.
  ⚠ **which puts this against the repo's own architectural stance.** AGENTS.md:
  *"LDtk owns world/level authoring."* A code-authored level is the exception, and
  it is exactly the exception that makes a block unable to carry art.
  ▢ **so the fork is real and it is Jon's** — raised as decision #14:
  * **(a) build B13's per-block art seam.** Code-authored levels stay first-class
    and gain art identity. Wider change, helps every game.
  * **(b) move Mary-O 1-1 to LDtk.** Aligns with the stated stance, makes the
